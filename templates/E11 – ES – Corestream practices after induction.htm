Corestream: quick operational guidelines for students

Goal: keep risk assessments moving quickly without compromising safety. Please read once, then use as a checklist before every new experiment.

Two things to remember
1) No approval = no lab work. Do not start work, and do not order new substances for the activity, until the RA is approved.
2) Corestream may not notify reviewers automatically, so you must email the approver/reviewer after submitting.

 

1) Know the workflow (what happens after you click "Submit")

- Student submits risk assessment (RA) + attachments.

- Reviewer(s) check the content and request edits if needed.

- PI/supervisor, or delegated approver, gives final approval.

- You start work only when the RA status is Approved.

Important: saving the RA is not the same as submitting it. Make sure you submit it for PI/responsible-person approval.

 

2) Before you submit (set it up so it can be approved fast)

Check the essentials first:

- Approver/PI: select your official PI/supervisor (the person responsible for the project).

- Registered persons: add the people who will actually carry out the work.

- Contributors/reviewers: add the people who will guide, oversee, or review the work in your lab area (e.g., day-to-day supervisor, lab contact, technicians).

- Location: state where the work will happen, including room/facility and any movement between spaces.

- Attachment: include a short step-by-step procedure so reviewers can understand the activity beyond the Corestream fields.

Also check, if relevant:

- Training or supervision needed before the work can start
- Fume hood, LEV, glovebox, or other engineering controls
- Lone working or out-of-hours restrictions 		- Shared-space risks, including other lab users or visitors
- Storage, transport, segregation, and labelling/signage
- Waste disposal, clean-up, and decontamination

Take into account that approval can be a lengthy process because everyone reviews these on top of their normal workload. Start the RA as soon as possible.

 

3) Attach the step-by-step

Attach a short procedure (1-2 pages is usually enough). Reviewers need to understand what you will do, in practical terms.

Include
- Step-by-step method (what happens first to last)
- Chemicals/materials + concentrations/amounts/scale
- Products/by-products, if relevant
- Key hazards and matching controls
- PPE + engineering controls (e.g., fume hood, LEV, glovebox)
- Waste streams + disposal route
- Storage, clean-up, and decontamination steps, if relevant
- Emergency actions related to the procedure (spill, exposure, fire, equipment failure) 		Avoid
- "Standard procedure" without details
- Missing quantities (risk often depends on scale)
- Copy-pasting hazards without matching controls
- Forgetting waste, storage, transport, or clean-up steps
- Forgetting to add the people actually doing the work as registered persons
- Starting work while the RA is still saved as a draft, pending, under review, or otherwise not approved

 

4) After you submit (do this immediately)

- Email the approver/reviewer directly because Corestream notifications may not be sent.

- Include: RA title, brief one-line description, location, and when you hope to start.

- Ask them to confirm receipt and to let you know when the RA progresses.

- Be ready to revise quickly if feedback comes back. Keep the procedure file editable.
